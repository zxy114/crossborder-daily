Cross-Border Daily

Global cross-border commerce and trade briefing
Report date: 2026-09-24 (Beijing time) · Coverage: prior 24–72 hours · Sources: Reuters, Digital Commerce 360, Cross-Border Commerce Association, WTO updates

1. EU customs overhaul reshapes parcel flows

PolicyEuropeCustoms

The European Parliament approved revised Union Customs Code rules for e-commerce. Non-EU platforms and sellers will increasingly be treated as responsible importers, with a new handling-fee regime for low-value parcels and an EU Data Hub for trade compliance.

Source: dig.watch

2. China-to-EU parcel volumes drop sharply after duty changes

DataEuropeLogistics

China-to-EU e-commerce shipments fell about 65% year-on-year in August, following the EU’s move away from duty-free low-value treatment and toward parcel-level charges. The shift appears to be rerouting demand toward local and regional sellers.

Source: Reuters

3. Malaysia moves toward licensing cross-border platforms

RegulationSoutheast Asia

Malaysian regulators are finalizing cross-border e-commerce platform rules and a broader e-commerce bill. The effort is driven partly by concerns over ultra-low-priced imports, though officials said the intended framework applies across platforms rather than targeting any single provider.

Source: LowYat

4. India tightens e-commerce pricing and disclosure rules

PolicyAsiaCompliance

Amended e-commerce rules will require discount claims to be benchmarked against the lowest price in the prior 30 days, clearer sponsored-listing disclosures, and stronger grievance mechanisms. The changes take effect January 1, 2027 and are pushing brands to rethink festive-season pricing.

Source: The Hindu BusinessLine

5. FedEx releases cross-border trade-navigation tools

LogisticsTooling

FedEx launched Global Trade Navigator features covering tariff lookup, duty estimates, customs-value review, and checkout-cost transparency. The suite is aimed at reducing surprises from shifting duties and customs requirements in international shipping.

Source: Digital Commerce 360

6. Shopify expands multi-currency payouts

PaymentsPlatform

Shopify Payments added multi-currency payout coverage for eligible merchants in Australia and France, building on earlier expansions in the US, Singapore, and Hong Kong. The change reduces FX conversion drag for cross-border direct-to-consumer sellers.

Source: Shopify Changelog

7. eBay unifies European marketplace structure

MarketplaceEurope

eBay consolidated its European marketplace operations under unified leadership. The change is intended to simplify cross-border selling for merchants and reduce fragmentation across EU markets.

Source: Cross-Border Commerce Association

8. Amazon tightens Pan-EU FBA listing requirements

MarketplaceFulfillment

Amazon continued expanding Pan-EU FBA requirements, including an active Netherlands offer mandate for Pan-EU products. The policy shift affects seller eligibility and fulfillment cost structures across European marketplaces.

Source: Cross-Border Commerce Association

9. WTO digital-trade framework moves forward in narrower form

WTODigital Trade

After the broader e-commerce moratorium lapsed, WTO members advanced a narrower plurilateral path for electronic-commerce rules and interim dispute-settlement-style arrangements. The outcome keeps digital-trade governance alive but in a more fragmented configuration.

Source: Inside U.S. Trade

10. Malaysia tightens cross-border e-commerce platform oversight

RegulationSoutheast Asia

Malaysian officials are coordinating a new licensing and regulatory framework for cross-border platforms, with work on a replacement e-commerce bill expected to finalize in 2026. The goal is stronger consumer protection and a level playing field for local retailers.

Source: Malay Mail

Selected takeaways

  • EU customs and low-value parcel policy is the strongest near-term driver of cross-border cost and routing changes.
  • Payment and fulfillment policies continue to favor sellers with multi-market infrastructure and compliant data practices.
  • Asia-Pacific regulatory activity is rising in both India and Malaysia, adding compliance complexity for D2C and marketplace sellers.
